Editorial Review Notes appear on the draft and are removed before publication.

Bottom Line Up Front

Two May 2026 themes converge: AI is now reshaping how training itself is designed, and training must teach soldiers and leaders to use AI within the law of war. Both land at the moment SAFTI City is operational and MMRC 2 is on the books.

Nine developments across nine outlets cluster on two shifts the six TDDG branches own. First, AI is moving from a topic of training into the substrate of training - NATO ACT's Audacious Training, RAND's AI-warfare synthesis, and Breaking Defense's Army cyber wargame all describe AI changing how exercises are designed, delivered, and adjudicated. Second, soldiers and commanders now need explicit competency in operating AI-enabled systems under the law of armed conflict - Lieber Institute, CSIS, and RAND all point to this as a curriculum problem. SAFTI City and MMRC 2 are the venues where both shifts will play out for the Army.

Why This Matters to Army Training

The training enterprise is entering a phase where AI changes the form of training, not just its content, and where compliance training shifts from doctrine memorisation to judgement under uncertainty. NATO has already transferred its AI-in-training programme into operational use through the Joint Warfare Centre. RAND, CSIS, and the Lieber Institute have published the doctrinal and legal frameworks. The six TDDG branches now own the translation: which simulations adopt AI design, which PME courses add data and law-of-war competencies, which doctrine notes capture concurrent build-and-train, and how SAFTI City and MMRC 2 are instrumented to support all of it.

Global Developments with Training Implications

NATO ACT moves AI-in-Audacious-Training from concept to Joint Warfare Centre Beacon Project

What happened — NATO Allied Command Transformation announced in 2026 that the AI in Audacious Training Beacon Project has transferred to the Joint Warfare Centre as of January 2026. The project applies AI to exercise design, scenario generation, and behind-the-scenes adjudication for major NATO exercises - including LOYAL LEDA 2026, which placed participants in a multi-domain environment spanning land, air, maritime, space, and cyber.

NATO has crossed the line from 'AI as a topic of training' to 'AI inside how training is built and run.' The reference architecture exists. Cap Dev (Sim) and Training Analytics jointly own the question: what would the same approach look like in SAFTI City and in Army constructive simulation?

Suggested follow-up — Cap Dev (Sim): scope a SAFTI City AI-in-design pilot using NATO's Beacon as the reference architecture. Training Analytics: specify the data products that an AI-designed exercise should produce. Doctrine Development: draft a short note on what AI-designed exercises imply for exercise-design doctrine.

Relevant branches: B1B3B6 · refs: natoact-audacious-ai-2026

MMRC 2 announced with MUM-T ranges; Steckler names the institutional failure modes

What happened — MINDEF announced MMRC 2 on 27 February 2026, operational by 2031, with two double-height ranges purpose-built for manned-unmanned teaming training. In May 2026, Jake Steckler in War on the Rocks documented the US Army's decade-long failed effort to integrate Apaches with Shadow drones, naming the failure modes as cultural inertia, talent-model mismatch, and feedback suppression - not hardware.

The Army has roughly five years to draft the MUM-T training doctrine and curriculum that MMRC 2 will use. The Steckler failure modes are the warning. Doctrine Development and Cap Dev (Land/Infra) jointly own the draft; Leadership Development owns the connected PME competency.

Suggested follow-up — Doctrine Development: draft the MUM-T training-doctrine note. Cap Dev (Land/Infra): specify the range-utilisation patterns for MMRC 2's double-height ranges. Leadership Development: scope the PME competency module for OCS or GKS CSC.

Relevant branches: B2B5B6 · refs: wotr-armyaviation-may2026

CSIS proposes treating PME as the data substrate for training Army AI

What happened — CSIS, in a May 2026 piece in its Next Army series, argues that US Army Training and Doctrine Command should serve as the Army's lead agent for collecting, curating, and analysing PME-generated data to support RLHF (reinforcement learning from human feedback) pipelines for Army AI. The reframing positions PME as both a teaching activity and a data-generating activity.

This is a structural reframe of what PME produces. If accepted, every command-and-staff exercise, every after-action narrative, and every officer's written deliverable becomes potential training data for Army-specific AI systems. The implications for Leadership Development (what gets graded), Training Analytics (data pipelines), and Doctrine Development (institutional role of TRADOC equivalents) are immediate.

Suggested follow-up — Leadership Development: assess the implications for OCS and GKS CSC graded deliverables. Training Analytics: scope what a PME-data pipeline would need to be safe, useful, and ethically sound. Doctrine Development: draft a short note on HQ TRADOC's institutional role if this reframe is adopted.

Relevant branches: B3B5B6 · refs: csis-brainofarmy-2026

Project Flytrap 5.0 is the exercise SAFTI City was instrumented for

What happened — US 2nd Cavalry Regiment ran Project Flytrap 5.0 at Pabradė, Lithuania, in May 2026, integrating over 20 pieces of counter-UAS and friendly-UAS equipment at troop and squadron level. The unit developed new TTPs for synchronising intelligence, fires, and maneuver under a multi-layered drone threat.

SAFTI City has 11,000 sensors and capacity for two concurrent battalion-level exercises. The same mixed-stack drone problem could be instrumented at SAFTI City to a fidelity the US 2nd Cavalry could not reach in a temporary range. Cap Dev (Sim) and Cap Dev (Land/Infra) co-design the exercise architecture; Training Analytics specifies the data product.

Suggested follow-up — Cap Dev (Sim): draft a SAFTI City Flytrap-equivalent exercise architecture. Cap Dev (Land/Infra): confirm the sensor configuration captures intel-fires-maneuver decision points. Training Analytics: specify the decision-point data product.

Relevant branches: B1B2B3 · refs: defone-flytrap-may2026

Lieber Institute frames AI counterfire as a law-of-war training requirement, not a doctrine memorisation problem

What happened — The Lieber Institute at West Point published the second part of its Fighting at Machine Speed series in 2026, arguing that personnel operating AI-enabled systems for counterfire decisions need training that goes beyond system operability to address how distinction and proportionality are integrated and updated. DoD Directive 3000.09 was reissued in early 2025 to require legal review of autonomous and semi-autonomous weapon systems; the training that flows from that requirement is now the open question.

Compliance training for AI-enabled systems is shifting from 'know the rules' to 'judge under uncertainty when the system is the rule-applier.' That is a competency design problem, not a curriculum-update problem. Learning Systems and Leadership Development jointly own how this enters PME and unit training.

Suggested follow-up — Learning Systems: scope a graded competency model for AI law-of-war judgement, with scenario-based assessment. Leadership Development: integrate the competency into command training at OCS and GKS CSC. Doctrine Development: draft a short note on training requirements consequent to any future Army equivalent of DoDD 3000.09.

Relevant branches: B4B5B6 · refs: lieber-machinespeed-2026

RAND's May 2026 synthesis: AI reshapes four essential competitions in future warfare

What happened — RAND published RRA4316-1 in May 2026, examining how artificial intelligence could reshape four essential competitions in future warfare. The report frames the open question as how forces should train both human personnel and AI tools so that the two reinforce strengths and offset weaknesses.

This is the strongest single open-source synthesis available right now on the human-AI training problem. Training Analytics and Doctrine Development should both treat it as required reading for the next round of branch planning. The framing - reinforce strengths, offset weaknesses - is the right design question for any Army AI training programme.

Suggested follow-up — Training Analytics: prepare a 1-page summary of the four competitions framed against current Army training programmes. Doctrine Development: assess which doctrinal questions the RAND synthesis closes and which remain open.

Relevant branches: B3B6 · refs: rand-aifourcomp-may2026

Continuous Transformation acquisition model tested at division scale (Ivy Mass, May 2026)

What happened — The US 4th Infantry Division's May Ivy Mass event puts the next-generation command-and-control (NGC2) prototype against a red team for the first time at division scale. The Continuous Transformation acquisition model has over a dozen vendors building the system while soldiers test it. NGC2 was tested in degraded, denied, intermittent, and limited (DDIL) conditions in Ivy Sting 4.

The Continuous Transformation model is the transferable lesson, not the specific NGC2 design. The Army's training enterprise faces the question: if fielding becomes concurrent with training, what changes for the six TDDG branches? Doctrine Development drafts the short note; Cap Dev (Sim) owns the DDIL standard for SAFTI City; Training Analytics owns the data fabric implications.

Suggested follow-up — Doctrine Development: two-page note on concurrent build-and-train. Cap Dev (Sim): DDIL standard as a configurable SAFTI City exercise parameter. Training Analytics: scope the data substrate that concurrent fielding requires.

Relevant branches: B1B3B6 · refs: defone-ngc2-ivysting-may2026

Cross-Cutting Themes

AI is moving from a topic of training into the substrate of training

Four developments converge here. NATO ACT has transferred its AI-in-Audacious-Training Beacon Project to the Joint Warfare Centre - operational. SOCOM is already using generative AI heavily for staff workflows. Breaking Defense reports the Army running AI cyber wargames with industry leaders to develop a risk-continuum policy for AI-agent autonomy. RAND's May synthesis names the human-AI training problem as one of four competitions that defines future warfare. The shift is from 'we train soldiers on AI' to 'AI changes how training itself is designed, delivered, and adjudicated.' Cap Dev (Sim) and Training Analytics jointly own the translation.

refs: natoact-audacious-ai-2026, defone-sof-ai-may2026, breakingdef-aicyberwargame-may2026, rand-aifourcomp-may2026

Training the AI-judgement competency is a curriculum design problem, not a doctrine update

Three developments name the same shift. The Lieber Institute argues that AI counterfire requires training that goes beyond system operability to integrate distinction and proportionality judgements - a graded competency, not a memorised rule set. RAND frames the human-AI training pairing as one of four essential competitions. CSIS proposes treating PME as the data substrate for Army AI - which makes graded judgement deliverables institutionally important in a new way. Learning Systems and Leadership Development jointly own the design problem; Doctrine Development owns the institutional question.

refs: lieber-machinespeed-2026, rand-aifourcomp-may2026, csis-brainofarmy-2026

Army training infrastructure landed before peer-force institutional learning caught up - the design window is now

Four developments describe the same gap from different angles. 2nd Cavalry developed drone-warfare TTPs in a temporary range with no native instrumentation. SOCOM and SOUTHCOM are formally asking for more contested-environment range authority. The US Army's manned-unmanned teaming effort failed for a decade on cultural and talent-model questions before any facilities opened. NGC2 is being validated through Continuous Transformation while training infrastructure tries to keep pace. SAFTI City is operational since October 2024 with 11,000 sensors; MMRC 2 is announced with purpose-built MUM-T ranges. The infrastructure is ahead of the doctrine, curriculum, and operator-pathway design needed to use it well. The six TDDG branches hold the rare advantage of designing those institutions before the building opens.
